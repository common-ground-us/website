1. How the pieces fit together

Five systems, loosely joined:

  • The website (common-ground.us) — the public home for the Platform, essay, pledge, and look-up tool. Lives in a GitHub repo; Cloudflare publishes it automatically.
  • Tally — hosts the actual forms embedded on the site (the Pledge + the three Contact forms) and sends the pledge welcome email.
  • The forum (Discourse) — where discussion happens; linked from the site's Discuss button.
  • Social — LinkedIn (Company Page + your personal profile) and X (@Common1GroundUS).
  • The working board — the private project tracker, mirrored to a page on the site so you can check it from your phone.

Day to day you mostly review and publish; the Website thread (Claude) does the edits and hands you a one-click .bat to push.

2. The website

How publishing works: the site auto-deploys from the GitHub main branch. The moment a change is pushed to GitHub, Cloudflare rebuilds and the live site updates in about 1–2 minutes. There's no separate "deploy" button.

The normal cycle

  1. Claude edits the site in the cloud and mirrors the changed files onto your computer (the local copy at …\Common Ground\website).
  2. You preview locally: double-click START_SITE.bat, then open localhost:3000. (The black terminal window looks frozen — that's normal. Don't close it while previewing.)
  3. You publish: double-click LAUNCH_TO_LIVE.bat. It shows exactly what will be published, then asks you to type YES. It commits and pushes to GitHub.
  4. Wait ~1–2 min, then check common-ground.us. Claude can verify the live pages for you.
First push of a session may open a browser to sign in to GitHub — approve it. That's normal and only happens occasionally.

Good to know

  • LAUNCH_TO_LIVE.bat publishes the whole site (everything Claude has staged). It only stages site code — your scratch scripts and backups are never included.
  • If a push ever seems to "succeed" but the change isn't live, the usual culprit is a stale .git\index.lock file in the repo. Tell Claude — it's a known, quick fix.
  • You never have to touch code. If something needs changing, describe it to the Website thread and review the result on localhost.

3. The working board (private page)

The board is a project tracker owned by the Launch thread. A copy is published to a private page so you can glance at it anywhere: common-ground.us/WorkingBoard/ (unlisted, hidden from search).

Updating the board on the site

  1. You (in the Launch thread) update the board, and Launch drops a fresh snapshot into the MobileSync folder.
  2. Claude (Website thread) copies that snapshot into the site page and re-adds the "hide from search" tag.
  3. You double-click PUSH_WORKINGBOARD.bat to publish it. Live in ~1–2 min.

So the trigger phrase is simply: "There's a new board version — please sync it." Claude handles the middle step.

Your phone home-screen icon — do you re-add it?

No — never delete and re-add. The icon points at the live URL. Just tap it and it loads the current page. If it looks stale, pull down to refresh, or close the app fully and reopen it. Because the site caches for offline use, a brand-new update can occasionally take one extra reopen to show. The icon itself never needs replacing.

The pledge welcome email

  • Sent by Tally, requires Tally Pro ($29/mo) — free Tally can't send respondent email at all.
  • Comes "from Keith Lietzke" via notifications@tally.so, reply-to keith@common-ground.us. The trust chart is a link, not an inline image (Tally can't embed images).
  • Edited in the Tally dashboard → the form → Integrations / Emails.

Managing forms & submissions

  • Everyday changes (wording, fields, the email, the completion message): the Tally dashboard.
  • Two helper scripts read your Tally key from the local .env.local so nothing secret is exposed: SHOW_TALLY_FORMS.bat (lists your forms + IDs) and INSPECT_TALLY_FORMS.bat (dumps a form's structure for Claude to review).
  • Clearing test pledges before you go wide: open the Pledge form's submissions in Tally and delete the test rows. (Ask Claude if you'd like a script to bulk-clean them.)
The Contact forms confirm with Tally's own "Thanks" screen shown right on the Contact page — the site menu stays around it, so it's not a dead end. That's intended.

6. Social — LinkedIn & X

LinkedIn

  • Company Page: linkedin.com/company/common-ground-us — the institutional presence; post 2–3×/week and pin the key post.
  • Your personal profile is the bigger lever — personal posts reach far more people. When you post about CG, tag the Company Page (type @Common-Ground.US and pick it from the list so it turns into a blue link).
  • Reply to comments in the first hour — early engagement drives most of the reach.
  • LinkedIn shows only the first ~2 lines before "…see more," so the opening line has to earn the click.

X

  • Account: @Common1GroundUS. Post threads with the "+" button, then Post all; pin the first tweet.
  • New accounts get low reach and occasional "post unavailable" filtering — normal. Verifying a phone/email (free, in Settings) helps; the paid checkmark isn't needed.

The Thank-You "Boost" buttons

After someone pledges, the Thank-You page invites them to reshare your launch posts. Those buttons point at specific LinkedIn and X posts. When you want them pointed at a new post, send Claude the new post URLs and it re-wires them (a quick site push).

7. Analytics

GA4 + Google Tag Manager are wired into the site through Cloudflare (set up by Moorthy). Traffic and pledge conversions flow into GA4 automatically. Full read access / reporting is pending Moorthy's handoff — until then, ask Claude what's known and it'll help interpret what you can see.

8. Secrets & safety rules

  • Never paste API keys, tokens, or passwords into a chat — not to Claude, not anywhere. Keys live only in .env.local on your computer; the helper .bat scripts read them there so they stay private.
  • Logins (GitHub, Cloudflare, Tally, DNS, Google/GA) are yours — keep them in your password manager, not in chat.
  • Claude does the edits; you do the pushes and any login. That split keeps control with you.
  • Don't collect sensitive personal info on public forms.
